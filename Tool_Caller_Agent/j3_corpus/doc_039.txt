**Threat Intelligence Note: LockByte Ransomware-as-a-Service Campaign Targeting Healthcare Organizations**

**Date:** October 25, 2023

**Campaign Overview:**
The LockByte ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) campaign is a sophisticated threat actor that has recently intensified its focus on healthcare organizations. This campaign leverages a modular and adaptable approach to maximize profit while minimizing the risk of detection. The primary targets are hospitals, clinics, and other medical institutions across North America.

**Affiliate Model:**
LockByte operates through an affiliate model, which allows it to expand its reach by partnering with less technically sophisticated individuals or groups who can execute the ransomware deployment. Affiliates receive detailed instructions on how to carry out their tasks, including phishing campaigns, initial access breaches, and subsequent lateral movements within the network. A key affiliate, "Dr. Alex," has been particularly active in recent weeks.

**Encryption Methods:**
The LockByte RaaS employs advanced encryption techniques to ensure that victims' data is inaccessible without a decryption key provided by the attackers. The ransomware utilizes AES-256 for file encryption and RSA-4096 for encrypting the private key, making it nearly impossible to decrypt files without paying the ransom. Additionally, LockByte has implemented a custom obfuscation layer that can evade common antivirus software.

**Negotiation Patterns:**
LockByte's negotiation process is often characterized by aggressive tactics aimed at maximizing the ransom amount while creating urgency for the victim organization. Initial contact is typically made through encrypted channels such as Telegram or Discord. The attackers demand an initial payment of $30,000 within 72 hours to avoid data loss. If this does not yield results, they threaten to leak sensitive patient information unless a higher ransom is paid.

**Tactical Mitigation:**
To mitigate the risk of falling victim to such campaigns, healthcare organizations should implement robust cybersecurity measures including regular backups, multi-factor authentication, and employee training on phishing awareness. Organizations are advised to establish clear incident response plans and maintain continuous communication with law enforcement and cybersecurity experts to stay ahead of emerging threats.

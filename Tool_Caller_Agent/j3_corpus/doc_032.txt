#### Indicators of Compromise:
1. **Unusual CA Issuance**: The certificate was issued by an unknown or untrusted Certificate Authority.
2. **Short Lifespan**: The certificate is nearing its expiration date, suggesting a recent compromise.
3. **No Public Trust**: Lack of verification and trust in the issuing authority makes it more likely to be used maliciously.

#### Threat Analysis:
The use of an unauthorized SSL certificate can allow attackers to intercept traffic between the user's device and the corporate network infrastructure, enabling them to read, modify, or steal sensitive information such as login credentials, financial data, and other confidential communications.

#### Remediation Steps:

1. **Certificate Revocation**: Immediately revoke any issued certificates from unknown CAs and ensure all legitimate certificates are up-to-date.
2. **Network Monitoring**: Implement advanced network monitoring tools to detect and alert on suspicious SSL certificate fingerprints or unauthorized connections.
3. **User Education**: Educate users about the risks of MITM attacks, particularly when using public Wi-Fi networks or unfamiliar devices. Encourage them to verify the authenticity of certificates before establishing secure connections.
4. **Network Policy Review**: Review and update network policies to enforce the use of trusted CAs for all SSL/TLS certificates issued within the organization.
5. **Security Audits**: Conduct thorough security audits to identify any vulnerabilities in your existing certificate management processes and implement necessary updates.

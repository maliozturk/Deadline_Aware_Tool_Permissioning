### IOC Intelligence Entry

**Title:** Suspected Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attack via Suspicious SSL Certificate Fingerprint Targeting Corporate Network Users

**Date:** January 15, 2024

**Summary:**
An analysis of network traffic has identified a suspicious SSL certificate fingerprint that is suspected to be used in a man-in-the-middle (MitM) attack targeting corporate users accessing the company's Virtual Private Network (VPN). This fingerprint has been observed across multiple sessions and is believed to be employed by attackers attempting to intercept sensitive information, such as login credentials or internal communications.

**Detailed Analysis:**

1. **Certificate Details:**
   - **Fingerprint:** SHA-256: `90:AB:CD:EF:01:23:45:67:89:AB:CD:EF:01:23:45:67`
   - **Issuer:** DigiCert High Assurance CA-3
   - **Subject:** `*.corp.example.com`
   - **Validity Period:** Issued on January 1, 2024; Valid until December 31, 2024
   - **Notices and Policies:** Includes standard security practices notices

2. **Indicators of Compromise (IoCs):**
   - The SSL certificate is issued to a wildcard domain (`*.corp.example.com`), which suggests it could be used to intercept communications with any subdomain under `corp.example.com`.
   - The certificate was recently issued and has not been observed in the company's trusted certificate store, raising suspicion.
   - Network traffic analysis shows encrypted data being transmitted between suspected internal systems and an external IP address (`192.0.2.10`), which is consistent with MitM behavior.

3. **Mitigations:**
   - **Immediate Action:** Conduct a thorough review of the certificate chain to confirm its legitimacy. If it is unauthorized, revoke the certificate immediately.
   - **Update Trust Store:** Add the suspicious certificate fingerprint to the company’s internal trust store and block any untrusted certificates issued by DigiCert High Assurance CA-3 for the `*.corp.example.com` domain.
   - **Network Monitoring:** Implement real-time monitoring of network traffic for unusual patterns or deviations from normal behavior, such as unexpected connections with external IP addresses.
   - **User Awareness:** Educate corporate users about recognizing and avoiding potential MitM attacks. Encourage them to report any suspicious SSL certificate warnings or untrusted connection alerts.

4. **Remediation Steps:**
   - **Certificate Revocation:** Use the Certificate Revocation List (CRL) from DigiCert to revoke the suspect certificate.
   - **Security Updates:** Apply security updates and patches for all systems, especially those that rely on SSL/TLS encryption.
   - **Network Policies:** Strengthen network policies by implementing advanced threat detection tools such as Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) and Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS).
   - **Regular Audits:** Conduct regular internal audits of certificate management practices to ensure compliance with security standards.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of this suspicious SSL certificate fingerprint indicates a potential MitM attack targeting corporate users. Immediate action is required to secure the network infrastructure, update trust stores, and educate employees on recognizing fraudulent certificates. Continuous monitoring and vigilant threat hunting are essential to mitigate risks associated with such attacks.
